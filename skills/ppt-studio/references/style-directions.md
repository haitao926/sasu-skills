# Style Directions

Use this reference when proposing or evaluating visual directions. Do not present these as fixed templates. Translate them into topic-specific proposal cards.

## Direction Families

`editorial-briefing`

Magazine-like but serious. Strong titles, large numbers, restrained charts, generous margins. Good for strategy reports, executive briefings, research summaries.

`academic-report`

Formal research presentation style. Uses section numbering, research questions, literature/policy grounding, methodology diagrams, evidence tables, limitations, and restrained citations. Good for academic reports, thesis defenses, teaching-research cases, conference talks, and application reports that must sound rigorous rather than promotional.

`technical-lab`

Precise, diagram-led, modular. Uses subtle grids, code/data motifs, system diagrams, instrument-like details. Good for AI, engineering, scientific, and product architecture decks.

`swiss-grid`

Strict Swiss International Style adapted from Guizang templates. Large thin typography, top-left titles, one accent color, hard grid, evidence images as rectangular proof blocks. Good for AI/product/engineering decks, case applications, and evidence-heavy reports. Read `template-systems.md` before using.

`magazine-eink`

Editorial magazine/e-ink style adapted from Guizang templates. Warm paper, quiet image spreads, strong quotes, chapter rhythm, and restrained texture. Good for narrative talks, industry observations, personal keynotes, and design/culture decks. Read `template-systems.md` before using.

`institutional-ceremony`

Formal, polished, identity-forward. Uses emblem/brand colors carefully, dignified spacing, chapter pages, and strong cover composition. Good for defense decks, school/company reports, awards, and public-sector presentations.

`venture-narrative`

Sharp and persuasive. Uses contrast, traction metrics, product shots, market maps, and claim-evidence rhythm. Good for pitch decks and roadshows.

`data-room`

Dense but readable. Uses dashboard-like compositions, tables, charts, callouts, and source notes. Good for analysis decks, business reviews, and financial/product metrics.

`visual-story`

Image-led and cinematic. Uses generated scenes, process imagery, full-bleed section dividers, and minimal text. Good when the deck must persuade emotionally or explain a concept visually.

## Proposal Card Shape

Each style option should include:

- name: short, client-facing;
- visual thesis: one sentence explaining the idea;
- best for: why it fits this topic/audience;
- risks: what might make it wrong;
- motifs: 2-3 concrete visual devices;
- preview files: cover, agenda, body image paths.

## Anti-Generic Rules

Reject or revise a direction if:

- it could fit any random corporate deck without changing a word;
- it relies only on gradients, glass cards, abstract waves, or generic tech lines;
- it has no content-specific motif;
- it cannot support both cover and dense body pages;
- it ignores the user's identity anchors.

## Style Lock

After the user picks previews, write `design_spec.md` with:

- chosen visual thesis;
- typography direction;
- color logic, not just colors;
- layout rhythm;
- image/diagram treatment;
- density rules;
- what must not change in later slides.
